1. Who we are and what this covers

VA Labs LLC publishes EOS³ Wallet (“we,” “us,” or “our”), a wallet application for the EOS network. This policy describes information handled by the wallet application, this website, and the EOS³ login, authorization, and resource-sponsorship services when you choose to use them.

Independent blockchain networks, node operators, DApps, authentication providers, app stores, and other third-party services also handle information under their own policies. Their involvement is described below.

2. Information handled by the wallet

Wallet information stored on your device
Imported private keys, locally generated device signing keys, wallet accounts and labels, preferences, manually entered contacts, and connection/session information are stored locally as needed for wallet operation. The wallet protects stored signing secrets using the device’s secure storage and, where applicable, encryption tied to your master password. Our wallet client does not send your imported private keys or master password to our servers or Firebase Analytics. Exporting or copying a secret at your request makes it available outside that protected storage.
Public blockchain data and transaction requests
Node and history services receive account names, public keys, balance and history queries, and signed transactions needed to retrieve information or broadcast your requested actions. Transaction data may include recipient accounts, amounts, contract actions, permissions, and memos. Resource-sponsorship and authorization services receive relevant accounts, transaction contents, authorization settings, request identifiers, and signatures to provide the feature you selected.
Optional sign-in, account creation, and authorization
If you choose Google sign-in, the related service receives the Google account identifier, email address and verification status, name, and profile image made available through the authorized sign-in flow. Login and account-linking services process session tokens, account associations, public keys, credential identifiers, registration/authentication challenges, and verification responses. If you use a Telegram-linked authorization flow, the service can associate a Telegram user identifier, username/display name, and the requesting project/bot with the authorized wallet account and permission settings. Passkey private credentials are managed by your platform or credential provider; any credential synchronization follows that provider’s settings and policies. We do not receive your biometric templates or Google password.
DApp and wallet connections
When you authorize a DApp connection or signing request, the selected DApp may receive your account name, public key, network, requested transaction/signature data, and connection status. Wallet compatibility interfaces can also expose the app version, language, operating system, and a random identifier generated for that site. Persistent ESR / Anchor-style sessions may send encrypted session messages through relay providers. Visiting a website in the DApp browser also makes an ordinary web connection to that site.
Technical, usage, and support information
Our services, hosting/CDN providers, and connected services may receive IP addresses, request times, user-agent/app version information, request results, and security or operational logs. Firebase Analytics processes the usage information described in section 4. If you contact us, we receive your contact address and the messages or files you choose to send. Apple may process TestFlight enrollment, device/build information, crash reports, and submitted feedback under its own terms; feedback and diagnostics made available to us are used to test and support the app.

Public blockchain records: account names, public keys, balances, permissions, transfers, and transaction memos can be publicly visible and retained by the network indefinitely. Do not put private information in a transaction memo. We cannot erase or make a confirmed blockchain record private.

3. Why information is used and who receives it

We use information to provide wallet and connected-service functions; authenticate and link accounts; process actions you request; protect services against abuse; diagnose problems; understand basic app usage; respond to support and privacy requests; and meet applicable legal obligations.

Information is shared according to the function used:

  • Blockchain, RPC, history, token and market-data providers: the queries and transaction data required for account, asset, resource, pricing, and transfer features.
  • Google: optional Google sign-in and Google Analytics for Firebase. See Google’s Privacy Policy and Firebase privacy information.
  • Apple and operating-system/credential providers: app distribution, TestFlight, device permissions, secure storage, biometric authentication, and passkey functionality, according to the selected platform’s policies. See Apple’s Privacy Policy.
  • Google ML Kit on Android: QR recognition is performed on the device; the SDK can process device/app information, installation identifiers, performance, and usage diagnostics as described in ML Kit’s terms and data disclosures. This Android SDK disclosure does not mean the iOS app uses Android ML Kit.
  • Hosting, CDN and security providers, including Cloudflare: network and request data needed to deliver and protect our websites and services. See Cloudflare’s Privacy Policy.
  • DApps, authorization, sponsorship, image/content and relay providers: information needed for the external resource or feature you use. A DApp is responsible for its own subsequent processing.

We do not sell personal information or use wallet transaction activity for targeted advertising. We use service providers under their applicable service and data-protection terms and expect appropriate confidentiality and security protections. We may disclose information where legally required, to address fraud or security threats, or as part of a lawful organizational transfer with appropriate protections.

4. Usage analytics and website technologies

Configured release builds use Google Analytics for Firebase to measure installations, sessions, engagement, and basic technical performance of app use. Collection is enabled by default after the app checks its saved preference. The SDK supplies automatic events such as first_open, session_start, and user_engagement, an analytics installation identifier, and standard device/app metadata. Network requests also disclose an IP address to the receiving service. This processing is not a claim of complete anonymity.

The wallet’s analytics integration does not send wallet account names, balances, public/private keys, master passwords, transaction details, signatures, DApp URLs, or a custom user ID. Advertising storage, ad user data, and ad personalization are disabled in the app’s configuration; IDFA/advertising-ID support is not used for this integration.

Current controls: this release does not offer an in-app analytics switch or a first-launch analytics prompt. Disabled preferences saved by an earlier version are respected. Contact us about analytics and privacy requests; we may need a relevant technical identifier to locate data, and cannot identify every analytics installation from an email address or wallet account. Removing the app does not itself erase previously collected analytics data.

Related sign-in websites use cookies and session/local storage to keep you signed in and complete authorization flows. Hosting/CDN systems can use security cookies and process website traffic/performance information. This privacy page does not embed app analytics, advertising scripts, or third-party fonts.

5. Permissions and device features

  • Camera and selected photos: used to scan a QR code or read a code from an image you select. Saving receive codes or selected DApp images may request permission to save to your photo library. The wallet’s QR scanning feature does not upload your full photo library or camera stream to us.
  • Biometric authentication: the operating system confirms authentication for unlocking or signing; the wallet does not receive your face or fingerprint template.
  • Clipboard and sharing: copying or exporting information uses the system clipboard or a destination you choose. Clipboard history, synchronization, screenshots, backups, and receiving apps may retain an exported secret. Automatic clipboard clearing is best effort.

You can revoke camera, photo and biometric permissions through device settings; related features may stop working. These operating-system permission controls do not serve as a Firebase Analytics switch. The wallet does not request microphone access, precise location, or access to your device address book for its core wallet features. Independent websites opened in the DApp browser have their own privacy practices.

6. Retention, deletion, and your choices

We retain off-chain information only as needed for the purpose for which it was collected, including operating an active linked account, completing a request, investigating abuse, providing support, and complying with legal obligations. When information is no longer needed, we delete it or de-identify it, subject to applicable retention requirements and backup lifecycles. Analytics retention is governed by the configured Google Analytics retention settings and Google’s applicable terms; non-identifying aggregate statistics may remain longer.

You can manage/remove local wallets and connections using the app’s available controls, revoke device permissions, and revoke Google authorization through your Google account. Before removing local keys, make sure you retain any recovery material you need. Removing a local wallet, uninstalling the app, or disconnecting a DApp does not automatically revoke an on-chain permission or delete information already received by another service. Secure-storage entries and backups may persist depending on platform settings.

For account-linked service information, support records, or other information under our control, email xiaojuzibtc@gmail.com with your request. Depending on applicable law, you may request access, correction, deletion, a copy/portability, restriction, or object to processing, withdraw consent where processing relies on it, and complain to a relevant data-protection authority. We may ask for proportionate verification of your request. Never send a private key, seed phrase, or master password to prove ownership.

We cannot delete public blockchain records, information controlled independently by third-party services, or data we must retain by law. For Apple/TestFlight or Google-held data, their own privacy and account tools may also be necessary. We will explain applicable limitations when responding.

7. Security and international processing

We use encrypted network connections, protected local secret storage, and access controls appropriate to the functions involved. No system can guarantee absolute security. You remain responsible for protecting your device, recovery material, exported keys, and the sites or apps to which you grant permissions.

Our infrastructure and third-party providers can process information in countries other than your own, whose laws may differ. We use the relevant providers’ data-protection terms and applicable safeguards for service processing. Contact us for questions about the processing relevant to your use of the app.

8. Children and policy updates

EOS³ Wallet is not directed to children. If you believe a child has provided personal information to us, contact us so we can investigate and take appropriate action.

We may update this policy as features, providers, or requirements change. The updated policy and date will be published at this address. Where required, we will provide additional notice or obtain consent; publication of this policy alone is not an in-app consent mechanism.
